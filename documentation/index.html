Project methods

Documentation

How concert programs become searchable, comparable records—and how the site preserves the relationship between standardized data and historical evidence.

Scope

The project documents performances of medieval and Renaissance music between 1915 and 1960. A concert enters the dataset when a surviving program or archival description provides enough evidence to identify the event and at least part of its repertory.

Data model

ProgramEvent, date, place, source, and scan
Performed itemOrder and wording in one program
PieceProvisional grouping of performed occurrences
PersonComposer, performer, or other role

Program records and their performed items are separate. This lets the site preserve the order and language of the historical document while linking repeated performances to standardized people and pieces.

Transcription and standardization

Titles and names as printed remain attached to each occurrence. Standardized records make variant spellings and translated titles discoverable without silently replacing the original wording. Uncertain readings and editorial additions are identified explicitly.

Identifiers

Concerts and catalogued people use stable identifiers where the source provides them. Each performed item retains its occurrence-level Piece ID. Piece pages currently use a provisional grouping of normalized program wording plus composer identifier or name; canonical work identifiers will replace that grouping when they become available.

Canonical piece linksOccurrence-level “piece IDs” imported from the working sheet are not treated as composition identifiers. Canonical piece records are created separately when the evidence supports a match.

Places and mapping

Venue and city names are recorded as they appear, then linked to normalized places with coordinates. The map uses Leaflet and OpenStreetMap tiles. Marker totals reflect the current filters and only include records with sufficiently precise coordinates.

Limitations

The corpus is shaped by archival survival, access, and the current state of cataloguing. Aggregate charts show patterns in the assembled dataset and should not be read as complete measures of historical concert activity.
